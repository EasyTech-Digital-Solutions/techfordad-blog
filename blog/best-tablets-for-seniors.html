For many families the goal is simple: video calls with the grandchildren. The tablet with the best specs is rarely the right choice for that, and this guide explains what matters instead.

A tablet can transform life for an aging parent: enabling FaceTime with grandchildren, reading books with large text, watching favourite shows, and even doing gentle brain training games. But the wrong tablet can become a frustrating paperweight collecting dust on the nightstand.

We researched six of the top tablets, focusing on three things that matter most to seniors and caregivers: screen readability, ease of first-time setup, and video call quality. Here's what stands out.

Quick tip: Fire tablets work best with Prime. Check whether your parent already has it, or start a free trial if they are eligible.

What to Actually Look For (Not What Specs Say)

1. Screen Size: Bigger is Usually Better

For most seniors, a 10"+ screen is the best fit. Large enough to read comfortably and video call with family, but not so heavy it's a burden to hold. The exception is seniors who move around a lot, for them, the iPad Mini's 8.3" portability wins. For seniors with macular degeneration or significant vision loss, the 10.9" iPad at maximum text size and maximum brightness is the best option on any platform. If your parent mainly wants to read books, a lighter e-reader may suit them better.

2. Accessibility Features

iOS (iPad) has the strongest built-in accessibility suite: including text size, display zoom, spoken content, VoiceOver screen reader, and Guided Access. Amazon Fire tablets have improved significantly with Alexa voice control. Android tablets are improving but still lag behind for senior-specific ease of use. If accessibility is the top priority, choose iPad first, Fire HD second, Samsung third. Our iPad setup guide for an elderly parent walks through the exact settings to change.

3. Wi-Fi vs. Cellular

Wi-Fi only tablets are cheaper and work fine for most home-based seniors. If your parent travels or moves between multiple homes, a cellular model is worth the extra cost, no need to find a WiFi network. The iPad Mini 5G is the best cellular tablet for seniors, but adding a cellular plan typically costs $10 to $15/month.

4. Cases and Stands

Always buy a protective case with a built-in stand. Seniors drop things, and a $30 case is far cheaper than a cracked screen. We recommend the Apple Smart Folio for iPad and the Amazon Kids Case (it works for seniors too. It's just well-built) for the Fire HD. A stand case means your parent doesn't have to hold the tablet during a 30-minute FaceTime call. For longer emails, pair the tablet with a large-button keyboard.

5. iPad vs Android vs Amazon Fire: The Honest Comparison

This is the question families ask most. Here is our direct, no-fluff answer based on how the three platforms compare:

  • iPad wins for: Accessibility depth, FaceTime simplicity, long-term software support (5+ years), and confidence-building for first-time users. If your parent has never used a tablet and you want the highest chance of success, buy an iPad.
  • Amazon Fire wins for: Voice-first users (Alexa removes the need to tap anything), tight budgets, and seniors who primarily want to watch content and video call via Zoom. The Fire HD 10 delivers 80% of the iPad experience at 40% of the price. See our best Alexa devices for seniors if voice control matters most.
  • Samsung Android wins for: Seniors who already own a Samsung Galaxy smartphone (familiar interface), those who need exceptionally loud speakers, and anyone who prefers Google services (Google Meet, Gmail, Google Photos) over Apple's ecosystem.
  • Avoid cheap Android tablets under $80: They run outdated Android versions, receive no security updates, and have screens too dim and slow to be enjoyable. The $40 price difference to a Fire HD 8 is always worth it.

6. Best Tablet for Video Calling with Family

Video calling is the single most important feature for most senior tablet users. Here's how each platform stacks up specifically for this use case:

  • FaceTime (iPad/iPhone only): The easiest video call experience available on a tablet. One tap on a contact photo starts a FaceTime call. No account setup required beyond an Apple ID. The recipient doesn't need to do anything except answer. For families where everyone has an iPhone, FaceTime on an iPad is unbeatable.
  • Zoom (all tablets): Works well on all platforms. Requires the Zoom app and a meeting link or scheduled call. Good for multi-person family calls. The Amazon Fire HD 10 with Alexa can start a Zoom call by voice: "Alexa, start a Zoom call with family."
  • Google Meet (Android/Samsung): Works best on Android tablets. If your family uses Google accounts, Meet is smooth and integrates with Google Calendar. Your parent's tablet shows "Family call at 3pm" and can join with a single tap.

Our recommendation for video calling: iPad + FaceTime if the family has iPhones. Fire HD 10 + Zoom or Alexa calling if the family has a mix of Android and Apple devices.

7. What Does a Senior Tablet Actually Cost?

The sticker price is just the beginning. Here's the full first-year cost for each platform:

  • Apple iPad 11th Gen: $449 tablet + $40 case + $0 software = ~$490. No subscription required for basic use. iCloud storage ($0.99/month for 50GB) is optional but recommended for photo backup.
  • Amazon Fire HD 10: $155 tablet + $25 case = ~$180. Amazon Prime ($139/year) dramatically improves the experience (Prime Video, photo storage, ad-free Fire) but is optional. Without Prime, the Fire HD 10 shows ads on the lock screen.
  • Samsung Galaxy Tab A11+: $299.99 tablet + $35 case = ~$335. Google One storage ($2.99/month for 100GB) recommended for automatic photo backup.
  • Hidden cost: cellular data. Adding a tablet to a family cellular plan typically costs $10 to $20/month. This is worth it for seniors who travel or have unreliable home WiFi.

8. Red Flags: Tablets to Avoid for Seniors

Across the devices we compared, these are the patterns that indicate a poor senior tablet:

  • Any "senior tablet" marketed with pre-installed simplified apps: These are typically cheap Android devices with a locked-down launcher that breaks within months and receives no updates. The better senior tablets are mainstream devices configured correctly.
  • Tablets running Android versions older than Android 12: Old Android versions mean no security patches, incompatible apps, and a frustrating experience as apps stop working.
  • Generic Chinese brand tablets under $80: Poor build quality, slow processors that make even video calls choppy, and displays too dim for comfortable reading. The Fire HD 8 at $129.99 is far superior.
  • Any tablet without Alexa, Siri, or Google Assistant: Voice control is the most important accessibility feature for seniors who struggle with touchscreens. Don't buy a tablet without it.
  • Tablets sold without a case: A dropped tablet is a broken tablet. Buy the case at the same time. Never set up a senior's tablet without putting it in a protective case first.

Setup Tips for a Senior's Tablet

  • Increase text size immediately: go to Settings → Display → Font Size and set it to Large or Extra Large
  • Set up auto-brightness: makes the screen readable in any light without fumbling with controls
  • Create a simple home screen: on iPad, remove all unused apps; put just 4 to 6 icons they'll use (Phone, FaceTime, Photos, Email)
  • Enable emergency contacts: set up Medical ID on iPhone/iPad with emergency contacts visible from the lock screen
  • Turn on Guided Access for dementia patients: locks the screen to one app to prevent confusion
  • Set a simple 4-digit PIN: avoid complex passwords; use fingerprint if available

Frequently Asked Questions

iPad vs Amazon Fire: which is better for seniors?
If budget allows, the iPad wins for most seniors due to better accessibility features, the FaceTime app (easiest video calling), and longer software support. However, the Amazon Fire HD 10 is a excellent choice for seniors who are more comfortable with voice commands via Alexa.
My parent has macular degeneration: which tablet is best?
The iPad with iOS accessibility features is our recommendation. You can enable "Zoom" (magnifier), increase contrast dramatically, invert colors, and use VoiceOver to have the screen read aloud. No other tablet matches this level of vision accessibility.
Can my parent use a tablet if they have arthritis?
Yes, with the right setup. The iPad Mini is the lightest and easiest to hold. Pair it with a stylus (seniors with fine motor difficulties often find a stylus easier than fingertip touch) and a stand case so they don't need to hold it at all during video calls.
What is the easiest tablet for a senior who has never used one before?
The Amazon Fire HD 10 with Alexa is often the easiest starting point for complete beginners because seniors can do everything by voice without touching the screen. Just say "Alexa, call [name]" or "Alexa, play [show]." For seniors willing to learn a touchscreen, the iPad is easier long-term due to better app design and faster performance.
Can a senior with dementia safely use a tablet?
Yes, with the right configuration. For dementia patients, the iPad with Guided Access mode is the safest option, Guided Access locks the screen to one app (like a video call or photos) so your parent can't accidentally work through away or make purchases. The Amazon Fire HD in Show Mode on a stand also works well for dementia patients who primarily need video calling and music, as it requires no interaction to display family photos and Alexa answers voice commands. Pair the tablet with a GPS tracker like AngelSense for dementia patients who wander.
Should I get WiFi-only or cellular for my elderly parent's tablet?
WiFi-only is fine for seniors who stay home and have reliable internet. Cellular is worth the extra cost (~$10 to $20/month on a family plan) if: your parent travels between homes or takes trips, your parent's home WiFi is unreliable, or you want them to be able to video call you from anywhere without troubleshooting connection issues. The iPad Mini 5G is the best cellular tablet for seniors who are active.
How do I set up a tablet so my elderly parent can video call me easily?
For iPad/FaceTime: Open Contacts, add family members with photos, then show your parent to tap the face to call. Add the FaceTime app to the first home screen position. For Amazon Fire + Alexa: Set up Alexa Contacts, then your parent can say "Alexa, call [your name]" from any room. For Android/Google Meet: Install Google Meet, schedule recurring calls in Google Calendar, and the tablet will show "Tap here to join family call" at the scheduled time, zero effort from your parent.
What is the cheapest way for grandparents to video call family?
The cheapest setup: Amazon Fire HD 8 ($130 one-time) + Amazon Prime ($139/year) + Alexa Contacts set up. Grandparents say "Alexa, call [name]" and the video call connects, zero screen interaction needed. Total first-year cost: ~$240. If family already uses iPhones or iPads, FaceTime is free, just buy a refurbished iPad (from $229 on Amazon) and add it to your Apple ID. WhatsApp and Google Meet video calling are both free on any device if the family has Android phones. The Alexa/Fire route is the single easiest option for seniors who struggle with touchscreens.
