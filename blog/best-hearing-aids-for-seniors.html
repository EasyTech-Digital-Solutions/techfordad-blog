Many older adults put off hearing aids for years because of the cost. Since over the counter hearing aids became legal in the US in 2022, prices have dropped a lot. This guide covers what is available without a prescription.

About one in three people in the U.S. between 65 and 74 has hearing loss, and nearly half of those older than 75 have difficulty hearing (NIDCD). Many wait years to seek treatment, and cost is a common barrier: prescription hearing aids often cost several thousand dollars per pair.

That changed in October 2022, when the FDA created an OTC hearing aid category, allowing adults 18 and older with perceived mild-to-moderate hearing loss to buy hearing aids directly, without seeing a hearing professional first. Prices now start under $300. We researched 7 of the most widely-reviewed OTC hearing aids to find the best options for seniors.

Signs of mild-to-moderate hearing loss (OTC appropriate): difficulty following conversation in noisy restaurants; asking people to repeat themselves; turning up TV volume; missing some words in conversation.

Signs that prescription hearing aids are needed: difficulty hearing even in quiet rooms; missing much of what people say; can't hear phone conversations clearly; hearing loss interfering with daily safety. Will Medicare Pay for a Hearing Aid?

Standard Medicare (Parts A and B): No. Original Medicare does not cover hearing aids or exams for fitting hearing aids, and you pay all costs (Medicare.gov).

Medicare Advantage plans: Sometimes. Some Medicare Advantage (Part C) plans offer extra benefits such as hearing, and the benefit and its dollar limit vary by plan. Call your parent's plan to ask about their specific hearing benefit.

FSA/HSA: ask your plan. Hearing aids and batteries count as medical expenses under IRS Publication 502; ask your FSA or HSA administrator whether your parent's specific purchase qualifies.

Medicaid: Varies by state. Some state Medicaid programs cover hearing aids. Contact your state Medicaid office or Area Agency on Aging.

Questions People Ask Before Buying

What is the best hearing aid for seniors in 2026?
For mild-to-moderate hearing loss: ELEHEAR Beyond Pro (~$599/pair) offers AI noise reduction and self-fitting via app with a Grade A result in HearAdvisor's lab test. Best value: Jabra Enhance Select M2 (~$1,695/pair) with 3 years of remote audiology care included. Budget pick: Audien Atom Pro 2 (~$289/pair) with no app needed. For moderate-to-severe loss or complex hearing profiles: see an audiologist for prescription aids.
What is the difference between OTC and prescription hearing aids?
OTC hearing aids (an FDA-regulated category since 2022) self-fit via smartphone app and cost $300 to $3,000/pair: appropriate for mild-to-moderate hearing loss, no audiologist needed. Prescription hearing aids come from a hearing health professional, often cost several thousand dollars per pair, and can be fitted for any level of hearing loss. If unsure which your parent needs, a hearing test (audiogram) from an audiologist is the right first step. See a doctor first for sudden or quickly worsening hearing loss, hearing loss in one ear only, ear pain, drainage or dizziness.
Does Medicare cover hearing aids?
Standard Medicare does not. Some Medicare Advantage plans include a hearing benefit, and the amount varies by plan, so call your parent's plan to check. Hearing aids and batteries count as medical expenses under IRS Publication 502; ask your FSA or HSA administrator whether a specific purchase qualifies. Some state Medicaid programs also cover hearing aids, contact your state Medicaid office to ask.
Are cheap hearing aids worth buying?
Under $100 "hearing amplifiers" (PSAPs) are not hearing aids: they are meant for people without hearing loss and are not regulated as medical devices by the FDA (NIDCD). Legitimate OTC hearing aids start around $289 (Audien Atom Pro 2) and include frequency-specific amplification for speech clarity. For anyone with real hearing difficulty, an OTC hearing aid is the right category to compare, not an amplifier.
How long do hearing aids last?
Most hearing aids last 3 to 7 years. Behind-the-ear styles tend to last longer than in-ear styles because electronics sit outside the ear canal (less moisture damage). OTC hearing aids with rechargeable batteries typically hold battery capacity for several years before needing replacement. Store in their charging case when not in use to maximize longevity.
