Caregivers often describe a new smartphone that ends up in a drawer because the touchscreen is confusing. Simple means something specific for older users, and this guide explains what to look for.

Finding the right phone for an elderly parent means balancing simplicity with features that actually help them stay safe and connected. Too complicated, and they won't use it. Too basic, and it won't meet their needs.

We researched 8 phones evaluated for senior usability: assessing setup complexity, call quality, button size, display readability, and emergency features based on verified caregiver and senior reviews. Here are the ones that stand out.

How We Ranked These Phones

Our rankings are based on manufacturer specifications, user reviews from verified senior buyers and their caregivers, accessibility feature documentation, and published comparisons from consumer technology outlets. Key criteria: button and display size, speaker volume ratings, ease of setup, emergency features, and carrier compatibility. We pay particular attention to reviews from seniors with arthritis, limited dexterity, or no prior smartphone experience, because that's who these phones need to work for.

Apple retired the iPhone SE name in 2025, replacing it with a more affordable "e" series: the iPhone 16e, and now the iPhone 17e, which arrived in March 2026. The 6.1" OLED display carries over from the 16e, and it's still a major upgrade over the old SE's small 4.7" LCD. The 17e adds Apple's newer A19 chip, MagSafe wireless charging (a first for Apple's budget iPhone line), and a 48MP rear camera. Apple Intelligence AI can read text aloud, summarize messages, and help with voice commands in ways that help seniors.

The critical tradeoff: the physical home button is gone. The 17e uses Face ID and swipe gestures, just like newer iPhones. For seniors who find gestures frustrating, this is a real downside. If your parent is upgrading from an older iPhone with a home button, expect a 1 to 2 week adjustment period. If they're brand new to smartphones, the 17e's gesture navigation can be taught cleanly without unlearning old habits. The older iPhone SE (3rd Gen, 2022, physical home button, 4.7" screen) can still occasionally be found refurbished through Amazon and third-party sellers if the home button matters more to you.

Our verdict: Best for seniors who want to video call family and are willing to learn Face ID gestures, or anyone upgrading from a gesture-based phone. If a physical home button is non-negotiable, look for a refurbished iPhone SE (3rd Gen) or the Jitterbug Smart5 instead.

What to Actually Look For in a Senior Phone

Button Size and Keyboard Layout

Seniors with arthritis or reduced dexterity need larger buttons with clear spacing. The Jitterbug Flip3 has the most senior-friendly physical keypad in its class, significantly larger than a standard phone. Not sure whether a flip phone or a smartphone suits your parent? Our Jitterbug vs iPhone comparison weighs them head to head.

Speaker Volume and Clarity

Many seniors have some degree of hearing loss. Look for phones rated at 85+ dB speaker volume and hearing aid compatibility ratings of M3 or M4, and T3 or T4 for telecoil compatibility. If a phone alone is not enough, see our guide to the best hearing aids for seniors.

Emergency Features

A dedicated emergency button that bypasses the standard dialing process is important for seniors who may panic in an emergency. The Jitterbug's Urgent Response button is the top choice here.

Screen Size and Text

For smartphones, look for displays 4.7" or larger with the ability to increase font size to "Extra Large" or "Accessibility" settings. The Jitterbug Smart5's 6.2" screen is particularly impressive for readability. Also check contrast, dark text on a light background is much easier to read than gray-on-white designs common in modern apps.

5. Network and Carrier Compatibility

Jitterbug phones work only with Lively's own service (Lively says the device cannot be used with other carriers), so check Lively's coverage map for your parent's address before you buy. The Doro 7050 and Samsung Galaxy A17 5G are unlocked and work on any carrier, giving you maximum flexibility. The iPhone 17e works on all major US carriers. Always check coverage for your parent's home address before purchasing. Reading from Canada? Carriers and prices differ there, so use our best cell phones for seniors in Canada and our guide to senior cell phone plans in Canada instead.

6. How Much Setup Help Will Your Parent Need?

Be realistic about how much ongoing tech support you can provide. If you live nearby and can pop over to help, an iPhone 17e with proper accessibility settings can work well. If you live across the country and your parent calls you every time something looks different on screen, the Jitterbug Flip3 or Doro 7050 will save both of you enormous frustration. Simpler is almost always better when you won't be there to help troubleshoot.

7. What Phone Features Seniors Don't Actually Need

Don't pay for features your parent will never use. Multiple camera lenses, 5G connectivity, 256GB storage, and thin/light premium designs are all marketed heavily but rarely matter for a senior phone. What matters: loud speaker, large text, physical buttons if possible, long battery, and a reliable emergency contact feature. Focus your budget on those things.

8. Red Flags When Buying a Senior Phone

  • Carrier lock-in with no coverage in your area: always check the network map first
  • Mandatory add-on plans for basic features: some phones require expensive bundles just to activate the emergency button
  • Touchscreen-only interfacesfor seniors with arthritis: physical buttons are significantly more reliable
  • Small charging ports or fiddly connectors: USB-C is easier than Micro-USB for older fingers

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the easiest phone for a senior who has never used a cell phone?
The Jitterbug Flip3 is the easiest starting point. It works exactly like the simple phones of the early 2000s (open to answer, close to hang up) with no confusing apps or touchscreen required.
My parent wants to video call the grandkids. Which phone should I get?
The iPhone 17e is the best for FaceTime with family, with a large 6.1" screen that makes video calls much clearer. Note it no longer has a physical home button. If that's a concern, the Jitterbug Smart5 supports video calling and is simpler to use for seniors who prefer a more guided interface.
Are Jitterbug phones worth the monthly plan cost?
Yes, for most seniors. Lively plans start at $14.99/month; the Preferred plan at $34.99/month includes the 24/7 Urgent Response feature, which provides reassurance well beyond what a standard phone plan offers.
What is the best phone for a senior with dementia?
The Jitterbug Flip3 is the safest choice for seniors with mild-to-moderate dementia. Its single-button answer/hang-up design (open to answer, close to hang up) is muscle-memory simple. You can pre-program just a few important contacts and lock out everything else. For advanced dementia, consider pairing a basic phone with a GPS tracker like AngelSense rather than relying on the phone for safety.
Can I add a senior phone to my existing family plan?
Yes, the Samsung Galaxy A17 5G and Doro 7050 are both unlocked and work on any carrier, so you can add them to an existing AT&T, T-Mobile, or Verizon family plan. The iPhone 17e also works on all major carriers. Jitterbug phones (Flip3 and Smart5) require a Lively plan, and Lively says the device cannot be used with other carriers. They cannot be added to another carrier's family plan.
How do I set up a phone for my elderly parent so it's easy to use?
For iPhones: go to Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size, enable Larger Text, Bold Text, and Increase Contrast. Set up Siri and show your parent how to press the side button to ask for help. For Android: most modern phones have an "Easy Mode" in Settings that simplifies the home screen. Pre-add emergency contacts, set up auto-answer for your own number, and remove any apps they won't use to keep the home screen clean.
What is the best cell phone plan for seniors on a budget?
The best value senior phone plans in 2026: (1) Consumer Cellular, from $20/month, uses AT&T and T-Mobile towers, no contracts, AARP member discount available; (2) T-Mobile 55+ plan: $27.50/line for two lines, best for couples; (3) Visible by Verizon: $25/month unlimited, no contract; (4) Lively (required for Jitterbug phones), from $14.99/month; the Preferred plan ($34.99/month) includes Urgent Response. For seniors who rarely use data and mainly call family, Consumer Cellular's entry plan is the best value. You pay only for what you use.
What is the best phone for an 80-year-old?
The Jitterbug Flip3 by Lively is our top recommendation for most 80-year-olds. It works exactly like the phones they grew up with, has consistently rated the loudest speaker in its class, and requires no learning curve. For 80-year-olds who are comfortable with technology and want video calls with grandchildren, the iPhone 17e with accessibility settings maximized is excellent. For 80-year-olds with cognitive decline or dementia, pair the Jitterbug Flip3 with an AngelSense GPS tracker: the combination gives family reassurance without overwhelming the senior with technology they may struggle to operate safely.
