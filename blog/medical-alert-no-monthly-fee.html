A monthly monitoring fee can be hard on a fixed income. This guide explains what no monthly fee really means and which options work without one.

We get it: medical alert systems feel expensive, especially on a fixed retirement income. A $40 to $50 monthly fee adds up to $500 to $600 a year for a device your parent may rarely use. So the search for "no monthly fee" medical alert systems is completely understandable.

Here's the reality: the monthly fee pays for a real human being who answers every time your parent presses that button, identifies themselves as a trained emergency operator, stays on the line, dispatches paramedics, and calls family members in sequence. That service cannot be free. But there are legitimate alternatives and low-cost options worth knowing.

The Truth About "No Monthly Fee" Medical Alerts

A monitored medical alert system requires a 24/7 staffed call center, with trained operators, backup power systems, redundant servers, and industry certifications. Operating this costs money, and that cost passes to subscribers. Any company advertising "zero monthly fee" with full 24/7 monitoring is either hiding the fee somewhere else (equipment rental, activation fees) or offering something that isn't true professional monitoring. See our best medical alert systems for monitored options at every price, or Life Alert vs Medical Guardian if you are comparing the two best-known brands.

That said, there are two legitimate paths for families who want to minimize ongoing cost:

  1. The Apple Watch SE: calls 911 directly with no monitoring center and no monthly fee (more models in our best smartwatches for seniors)
  2. Lowest-fee monitored systems: Lively Mobile2 starts at ~$24.99/month, Bay Alarm Medical's mobile plan at ~$34.95/month

Does Medicare Cover Medical Alert Systems?

Standard Medicare (Parts A and B): No. Original Medicare does not pay for medical alert systems (NCOA).

Medicare Advantage plans: Sometimes. Many Medicare Advantage (Part C) plans include supplemental benefits that can include medical alert devices or a monthly allowance. This varies significantly by plan and carrier. To check:

  1. Look at your parent's plan's Evidence of Coverage document under "Supplemental Benefits"
  2. Call the plan's member services number on the back of the insurance card
  3. Check Medicare's plan finder at medicare.gov and filter for plans with "Personal Emergency Response Systems"

Medicaid: Varies by state. Most Medicaid programs do not cover medical alert systems, but some states differ. Contact your state's Medicaid office or Area Agency on Aging.

FSA/HSA: Ask your FSA or HSA administrator whether a medical alert system qualifies. Rules differ by plan, and a doctor's note may be needed.

Honest Answers to Common Questions

Is there a medical alert system with no monthly fee?
True 24/7 monitored medical alert systems all have monthly fees. The closest alternative with no ongoing fee is the Apple Watch SE, which calls 911 directly using Emergency SOS with no monitoring center, one-time cost only. Among monitored systems, Lively Mobile2 starts at $24.99/month: the lowest fee available for monitored mobile protection. Bay Alarm Medical's mobile plan starts at $34.95/month.
What is the cheapest medical alert system per month?
Lively Mobile2 starts at $24.99/month (mobile GPS; fall detection +$9.99/month). Bay Alarm Medical's mobile plan starts at $34.95/month (mobile GPS; fall detection about +$10/month). Medical Alert Classic starts at $29.95/month (home only, fall detection extra). All three are month-to-month with no contract.
Can Apple Watch replace a medical alert system?
For healthy, tech-comfortable seniors: yes: the Apple Watch SE detects falls and calls 911 automatically with no monthly fee. For frail seniors, those with dementia, or anyone who needs a trained human operator to stay on the line and contact family: a dedicated monitored system is more reliable. The key difference is the Apple Watch calls 911; a medical alert system has a human being who speaks with your parent, assesses the situation, and dispatches the right response.
Does Medicare pay for medical alert systems?
Original Medicare does not, according to NCOA. Some Medicare Advantage plans include medical alert devices as a supplemental benefit, so call your parent's plan to ask. Ask your FSA or HSA administrator whether a medical alert system qualifies, because rules differ by plan. Most Medicaid programs do not cover them, though some states differ, and some nonprofit organizations offer help.
What happens if a senior can't afford a monthly medical alert fee?
Options: (1) Apple Watch SE, $249 one-time, no monthly fee, calls 911 directly; (2) check Medicare Advantage plan for supplemental benefit; (3) contact your state's Area Agency on Aging. Some programs subsidize devices for low-income seniors; (4) ask your FSA or HSA administrator whether a system qualifies; (5) Lively Mobile2 at $24.99/month is the lowest cost for full professional monitoring with GPS and fall detection included.
