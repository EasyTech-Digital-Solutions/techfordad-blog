Many families only start looking for a medical alert system after a fall. This guide is meant to help you choose before that happens.

When a parent or loved one falls or has a medical emergency, every second counts. A medical alert system can get help to someone faster after a fall or emergency. But with dozens of options on the market, and prices ranging from $20 to $60+ per month, choosing the right one is overwhelming.

We researched 12 of the most widely-reviewed medical alert systems on the market, comparing manufacturer specifications and monitoring contract terms. Here's what the research shows.

What to Actually Look For Before You Buy

1. At-Home vs. Mobile Systems

At-home systems work within a set range of a base unit. Mobile systems have built-in GPS and cellular, allowing seniors to get help anywhere. If your parent leaves the house regularly, choose a mobile system.

2. Fall Detection: Is It Worth It?

Falls are the leading cause of injury death among adults 65 and older, causing over 43,000 deaths in 2024 (CDC). Automatic fall detection means the system alerts responders even if your loved one is unconscious and can't press the button. We strongly recommend it for anyone who lives alone.

3. Monitoring Response Time

Response time can differ between companies, and the figures companies publish are their own claims: Medical Guardian's page says "response times as fast as 8 seconds." We found no independently tested response times, so ask each company how it measures response time and test the button during any trial period.

4. Contracts and Cancellation

Think twice about any company requiring a long-term contract. Most of our top picks advertise month-to-month service, but cancellation terms differ: Medical Guardian says it charges no cancellation fees, but its MGHome Cellular page carries a footnote saying fees may apply and its policies page charges for lost or damaged equipment, so ask every company for its terms in writing. Your parent's needs may change, so a multi-year contract can become a financial trap.

5. Is Life Alert Worth It?

Life Alert is the most recognized brand in medical alert systems, their "I've fallen and I can't get up" commercials have run for decades. But brand recognition is not the same as value. Life Alert does not post prices online (Life Alert's site says to call for a quote). Here is what independent and company sources say; see our full Life Alert vs Medical Guardian comparison:

  • NCOA reports a three-year contract for Life Alert, while most of our top picks advertise month-to-month service.
  • NCOA lists Life Alert plans from $69 to $109.85 a month, with a $248 activation fee for the base system.
  • Fall detection: NCOA reports that Life Alert does not offer it, and Life Alert's site describes a button you press to reach a dispatcher. Confirm with the company what your parent's plan includes.
  • Family app: ask Life Alert whether family members can get alerts or location updates; several competitors, including Medical Guardian, offer an app.

Our recommendation: on the published terms we found, Medical Guardian and Bay Alarm Medical list lower prices than Life Alert and avoid a three-year contract. We have not tested any of these services.

6. Signal Range and Cellular Coverage

At-home base units typically cover 600 to 1,000 feet, enough for most homes and yards. Mobile systems depend on cellular networks. Medical Guardian and Medical Alert Classic use AT&T; Bay Alarm Medical uses both AT&T and Verizon (automatically selecting the stronger signal). Before purchasing, check coverage maps for your parent's address, and note which carrier the device uses. In rural areas, the dual-network Bay Alarm Medical has a meaningful advantage.

7. Total First-Year Cost

The advertised monthly rate is rarely the whole picture. Add up: equipment/device fee, activation fee, shipping, and the monthly plan for 12 months. Bay Alarm Medical charges $0 activation and free shipping; Medical Guardian charges $0 to $149 depending on the package. Some companies advertise $19.95/month but add $99 in setup fees. Always ask for the full first-year cost in writing before committing. If the monthly fee itself is the dealbreaker, see our honest guide to no-monthly-fee options for the lowest-cost alternatives.

8. Red Flags: What to Avoid

  • Contracts longer than month-to-month: the industry standard is now no contract required
  • Response time not publicly advertised: companies hiding this number typically have slow centers
  • Overseas monitoring centers: in a crisis, language barriers or background noise can delay help
  • No fall detection option: any credible provider should offer this, even as an add-on
  • High-pressure phone sales tactics: reputable companies let you research and decide on your own timeline
  • No money-back trial period: top companies offer 30-day returns; avoid any that don't

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my parent need a landline for a medical alert system?
No, all five of our top picks work on cellular networks with no landline required. This makes them much simpler to set up and use in any modern home.
How accurate is automatic fall detection?
No system is 100% accurate, rapid sitting movements can sometimes trigger false alarms, so read each maker's own fall detection limits before you buy.
Will Medicare cover a medical alert system?
Original Medicare (Parts A and B) does not pay for medical alert systems, according to the NCOA. Some Medicare Advantage plans partially or fully cover them, and most Medicaid programs do not, though it varies by state. Check your parent's plan or contact the plan provider directly.
Is Life Alert the best medical alert system?
Life Alert is the most advertised, but advertising is not evidence of quality. On the published terms we found, Medical Guardian and Bay Alarm Medical list lower prices and avoid Life Alert's three-year contract (per NCOA). We have not tested any of these services, so confirm prices and terms with each company.
What is the best medical alert system for a senior living alone?
Medical Guardian MGHome Cellular is our top recommendation for seniors who live alone. It offers automatic fall detection as an add-on, so help can be dispatched even if your parent is unconscious and can't press the button. Fall detection is essential, not optional, for anyone living alone.
Can my parent wear a medical alert device in the shower?
Most can be, and a wearable that stays on in the shower is useful because wet floors are a common fall risk. Waterproof ratings differ by device, so check the maker's page for the exact rating before your parent wears one in the shower; the Lively Mobile2 is rated IP67.
Can an Apple Watch replace a medical alert system for seniors?
For healthy, tech-comfortable seniors: yes, the Apple Watch SE 3rd Gen is a genuine option. It detects falls and calls 911 automatically. For frail seniors, those with dementia, or anyone who needs a dedicated professional response: no. The Apple Watch requires the iPhone to be nearby or set up correctly. A dedicated medical alert has a monitoring center that speaks directly through the button device, identifies itself to emergency services as a medical alert, and contacts your family list in order, capabilities the Apple Watch doesn't replicate. Think of the Apple Watch as a health companion and the medical alert as an emergency lifeline.
How much do medical alert systems cost?
Monitored medical alert systems in our comparison range from $24.99/month (Lively Mobile2) to $34.95/month (Medical Guardian and Bay Alarm Medical, both with GPS). Automatic fall detection typically adds $5 to 10/month on top of the base price. Most families land in the $27 to 35/month range for a fully-monitored system with fall detection included. The only way to avoid a monthly fee entirely is a non-monitored option like the Apple Watch SE, which costs nothing per month but calls 911 directly instead of a trained monitoring center.
