Best Home Security Systems for Seniors in Canada 2026: Reviewed & Ranked

We compared the top home security systems available across Canada, with CAD pricing, no-contract options, insurance discount tips, and which systems work best for elderly parents living alone.

Setting up home security for a parent from a distance is harder in Canada, because many systems reviewed in US articles are not sold here. This guide covers what is actually available to Canadian families.

Home security in Canada is a different market from the US. Popular American brands like Vivint operate here, but others are US-only. Quebec has its own provincial regulations that restrict some monitoring services. And the big Canadian telecoms (Telus, Rogers, Bell) all have their own home security arms that compete with independent brands.

For seniors living alone, home security is about more than burglary protection. The right system provides a check-in through the monitoring centre, smoke and carbon monoxide detection, and often a medical pendant add-on that effectively doubles as a personal emergency response system. This guide covers the systems that actually make sense for aging Canadian parents, and the ones to skip.

Telus SmartHome Security is the dominant professional home security brand in Canada. ADT's Canadian operations merged into Telus, so when you sign up for Telus SmartHome, you're getting the equipment and monitoring infrastructure of a company that has been operating in Canada for over 150 years, now backed by one of Canada's largest telecoms.

For seniors, the most useful features are the optional medical pendant add-on (a wearable panic button with two-way voice, roughly $5/month extra) and the fact that professional monitoring connects your parent to a staffed Canadian monitoring centre rather than calling 911 directly. When the monitoring centre calls, they can speak with your parent, assess the situation, call a designated family member, and only dispatch emergency services if needed. This avoids false alarm dispatches and is far more appropriate than a system that dials 911 automatically.

Telus SmartHome plans include smoke, carbon monoxide, and optional flood monitoring. The touchscreen control panel is straightforward to arm and disarm. Voice control through Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant means a senior can arm or disarm the system without needing to walk to the panel.

Discount tip for families: If your parent already has a Telus or Koodo mobile plan, or you can bundle their home internet with Telus, the $10/month mobility bundle discount makes a meaningful difference over a multi-year term. Always ask about current promotional pricing, Telus frequently runs offers that reduce the upfront equipment cost.

The main drawback is the long-term contract. A 3-year or 5-year commitment means you need to be confident the system will work for your parent's situation before signing. Read the cancellation policy carefully, early termination fees can be substantial. For families who want flexibility, see Ring Alarm or Abode below.

Ring Alarm is Amazon's home security system, and it's available and supported across Canada: except Quebec, where Ring's monitoring service is not offered due to provincial government regulations. For families outside Quebec who want professional monitoring without being locked into a multi-year contract, Ring is one of the most attractive options.

The system is designed for DIY setup. A starter five-piece kit (base station, keypad, contact sensor, motion detector, and range extender) typically runs $200 to $350 CAD on Amazon.ca. Larger kits covering more doors and windows cost up to $600 CAD. Professional monitoring is available month-to-month for approximately $15 to 20/month CAD, significantly less than Telus SmartHome's professional monitoring tier.

For senior parents, the practical setup scenario is usually this: an adult child installs the system during a visit, sets up the keypad arming code, and connects the Ring app on both their own phone and (optionally) on a tablet in the parent's home for easy status checking. Ring doesn't offer a medical pendant, so if your parent needs both home security and a personal emergency button, you'd need a separate medical alert system like Telus LivingWell or MedicAlert Canada.

Ring's integration with Ring Video Doorbells is an added benefit. If you're also looking to add a video doorbell to help your parent see visitors safely, a Ring doorbell pairs smoothly with the Ring Alarm system.

If your parent already has Rogers internet, TV, or mobile service, Rogers Smart Home Monitoring is worth a conversation. Rogers offers wireless home security backed by their cellular network, with professional 24/7 monitoring and two-way voice built into the control panel. The main appeal is bundling: existing Rogers customers can often negotiate a package that combines home internet with home security monitoring at a reduced overall cost.

Rogers coverage is strongest in Ontario and British Columbia, and in major cities across Canada. In rural Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and Atlantic Canada, Telus's network typically has better reach, making Telus SmartHome the safer choice for parents in smaller communities or rural settings.

Before signing up, ask Rogers for the full term length and cancellation policy, like Telus, Rogers home security plans typically involve a multi-year commitment. Compare the bundled pricing carefully against what you'd pay with an independent provider.

Canada has dozens of regional home security companies that often fly under the radar of national comparison sites. Professional installation also matters for senior parents: a technician who can explain the system face-to-face, position sensors correctly, and set up the keypad at an accessible height is valuable. To find a reputable local provider, search your province name plus "ULC certified home security" or ask your home insurer directly which monitoring companies qualify for their maximum discount. Check reviews on Google and the Better Business Bureau before signing any contract.

Canada Home Security Buying Guide for Families

Professional monitoring vs. self-monitoring

For seniors living alone, professional monitoring is strongly recommended over self-monitoring. Here's why: with self-monitoring, the only person who gets the alert is whoever installed the app. If your parent's smoke detector triggers at 3am and you're not watching your phone, nothing happens. With professional monitoring, a staffed Canadian monitoring centre receives the alert, calls your parent, assesses the situation, calls you, and dispatches emergency services if needed, all automatically, in sequence. This is the key difference for a senior living alone.

Professional monitoring typically costs $28 to $55/month CAD depending on the provider and plan tier. Given that a professionally monitored system also qualifies for home insurance discounts of 5 to 20%, the net cost after the insurance saving is often lower than it appears at first.

What sensors matter most for seniors

  • Smoke and carbon monoxide detectors: more important than motion sensors for senior safety. Ensure your parent's system includes monitored smoke and CO protection, not just intrusion detection.
  • Door and window contact sensors: alert the monitoring centre if a door is forced open. Also useful for families tracking a parent with early dementia who may wander.
  • Medical pendant / panic button: Telus SmartHome offers this as an add-on for around $5 CAD/month. It gives your parent a wearable button to call the monitoring centre directly if they fall or feel unsafe.
  • Motion detectors: useful for intrusion detection, but configure pet-immune sensors if your parent has a dog or cat to avoid false alarms.
  • Flood sensors: worth considering for basement-level living or older homes; a burst pipe can cause enormous damage if undetected.

Key questions to ask any provider

  • Is your monitoring centre ULC-certified? (Essential for maximum insurance discount)
  • Where is the monitoring centre located: is it Canadian-staffed?
  • What is the minimum contract term, and what are the cancellation fees?
  • Is smoke and carbon monoxide monitoring included, or an add-on?
  • Does the system have a cellular backup if the internet goes down?
  • What happens during a power outage: how long does the backup battery last?
  • Can a family member be added as a secondary contact and have app access?

Landline vs. cellular systems

Many Canadian seniors still have a landline. Older security systems used the landline to communicate with the monitoring centre: a significant vulnerability, since a burglar who cuts the phone line disables the alarm. Modern systems communicate over cellular or broadband, making landline-cutting irrelevant. When shopping, confirm the system uses cellular backup so it stays connected even if the internet is down or a line is cut.

Home Insurance Discounts: What Canadian Families Need to Know

A professionally monitored home security system is one of the few home upgrades that actively lowers your insurance premium, and for seniors on fixed incomes, this matters.

How much can you save?

  • Professional monitoring: some insurers offer a premium discount for a monitored alarm system, and the amount varies by insurer and province. The Insurance Bureau of Canada says protective devices such as burglar alarms can potentially reduce your premiums and advises you to contact your insurer.
  • Non-monitored alarm systems (sirens only, no monitoring centre): some insurers give a smaller discount or none, so ask before you rely on one.
  • Other discounts: some insurers also offer age-based or retiree discounts. Ask whether they can be combined with an alarm discount.

How to claim the discount

After installing a professionally monitored system, ask your security provider for a Certificate of Monitoring (also called an alarm monitoring certificate). Submit this to your home insurer. They will apply the discount to your next renewal or, in some cases, mid-policy. Keep the certificate current, insurers may ask for it annually.

Ask your insurer for the exact dollar discount before you sign a monitoring contract, so you can compare it with the monitoring cost.

Provincial Differences

Home security regulations and available services vary by province. Here are the key differences families should know:

  • Quebec: Ring Alarm and Abode's professional monitoring services are not available due to provincial regulations. Quebec residents should use Telus SmartHome, Rogers Smart Home Monitoring, or a local ULC-certified provider. Many Quebec-based companies (including regional Videotron-affiliated services) fill this gap. If your parent's first language is French, confirm that the monitoring centre offers service en français.
  • Ontario: Ontario has no provincial subsidy specifically for home security systems for seniors, but home care coordinators through Ontario Health at Home can sometimes connect families with programs for seniors living independently. Ask your insurer whether a monitored alarm earns a home insurance discount.
  • British Columbia: BC has good Telus SmartHome coverage given Telus's strong network presence. ICBC home insurance is not available (BC's auto crown corp doesn't cover homes), so private insurers' discount programs apply. Rural BC areas benefit from Telus's network coverage.
  • Alberta: Ask your Alberta insurer whether a monitored alarm earns a discount. Alberta Continuing Care programs support eligible seniors: a home care coordinator can advise whether any home safety funding applies. Ring and Abode are both available.
  • Atlantic Canada (NS, NB, PEI, NL): All major options (Telus, Ring, Abode) are available except for Ring's Quebec restrictions. Local providers like ADT-affiliated dealers are common. Rural coverage may favour Telus for cellular backup reliability.
  • Manitoba and Saskatchewan: Both provinces have regional security companies and standard availability of Ring, Abode, and Telus. Manitoba previously had a residential security rebate program, verify current status with the provincial government before assuming it is still active, as these programs change.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best home security system for seniors in Canada?
Telus SmartHome Security is the most widely recommended option for Canadian seniors who want full professional monitoring. It's available in all provinces, offers a medical pendant add-on, and its monitoring infrastructure is backed by 150 years of ADT experience. For families who want no long-term contract, Ring Alarm (available outside Quebec) is an excellent lower-cost alternative with month-to-month professional monitoring.
Does a home security system lower home insurance in Canada?
Often, but it varies by insurer and province. You need to provide your insurer with a Certificate of Monitoring from your security company to claim the discount. Some insurers ask for ULC-certified monitoring before they give a discount, so ask yours which certificate it wants.
Is Ring Alarm available in Quebec?
No. Ring does not offer professional monitoring in Quebec due to provincial regulations. Quebec residents should look at Telus SmartHome Security, Rogers Smart Home Monitoring, or local ULC-certified Quebec-based providers.
What home security features matter most for elderly parents living alone?
The most important features for seniors are: 24/7 professional monitoring (not DIY-only), a simple keypad that's easy to arm and disarm, a medical pendant or panic button add-on, smoke and carbon monoxide monitoring, cellular backup (so the system works during internet outages), and remote app access for adult children. Two-way voice at the panel is also very useful so a senior can communicate with the monitoring centre without picking up a phone.
Can I get a home security system without a long-term contract in Canada?
Yes. Ring Alarm and Abode both offer month-to-month professional monitoring with no long-term contract. Telus SmartHome and Rogers typically require a 3-year or 5-year commitment. If your parent's living situation may change (moving to assisted living, for example) a no-contract system offers more flexibility.
What is a ULC-certified monitoring station?
ULC (Underwriters Laboratories of Canada) certification is an independent audit confirming that a central monitoring station meets rigorous Canadian safety and reliability standards. Some Canadian insurers ask for ULC-certified monitoring before they give a discount. When evaluating any monitoring provider, ask specifically whether their central station holds a ULC certificate of registration.

Choosing the Right System for Your Canadian Parent

For most families helping an aging parent stay safely in their home, the choice comes down to two questions: How important is professional monitoring quality, and how flexible does the commitment need to be?

Telus SmartHome Securityis the best choice for families who want the most reliable, full-featured professional monitoring available across Canada, especially for parents in rural areas or with complex monitoring needs like a medical pendant add-on. Budget for the 3 to 5 year term and make sure it fits your parent's timeline. Ring Alarm is the best choice for families outside Quebec who want professional monitoring without a long-term contract and are comfortable with DIY setup. Abode is the pick for Apple households. Local ULC-certified providers are worth investigating if the primary goal is maximizing the home insurance discount.

Whichever system you choose, professional monitoring with smoke and carbon monoxide detection is the baseline, not a luxury, for a senior living alone. If your parent also needs a wearable emergency button, pair the security system with a dedicated medical alert system like Telus LivingWell or MedicAlert Canada.
