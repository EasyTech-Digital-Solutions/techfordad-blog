My dad lives alone two states away, and the thing that finally convinced him to get a security system wasn't a burglary scare — it was a carbon monoxide close call at a neighbor's house. He didn't want cameras everywhere or a system he couldn't operate. He wanted something simple that would call for help if something went wrong, and that would let me know what was happening from a distance. This guide is for every family in that same position.

For a senior living alone, a home security system does more than deter burglars. The right setup provides monitored smoke and carbon monoxide detection, a simple way to call for help, and — just as important for adult children — a window into what's happening at the house from anywhere in the world. If you've already looked at video doorbells or smart home devices for a parent's house, a full security system is the next logical layer: it covers doors, windows, smoke, carbon monoxide, and emergency response in one connected package.

It's also worth being clear about what a home security system is not. It protects the property and can summon help, but it is not a substitute for a dedicated medical alert device. If your parent has a fall risk, pair whichever system you choose here with a wearable pendant from our best medical alert systems guide — the two categories work together, not as replacements for each other.

We researched seven of the most widely reviewed home security systems on the market in 2026, evaluating pricing, contract terms, ease of installation, senior-specific features like panic buttons and pendant add-ons, and how well each system supports remote monitoring by family members. What to Look For When Buying a Home Security System for a Senior

1. Professional Monitoring vs. Self-Monitoring — The Single Most Important Decision

For a senior living alone, professional monitoring should be considered the baseline, not an upgrade. With self-monitoring, an alert only reaches whoever happens to have the app open — if a smoke detector triggers overnight and no one is watching a phone screen, nothing happens next. With professional monitoring, a staffed center receives the signal, attempts to reach your parent, contacts a designated family member, and dispatches emergency services if needed, all in sequence and without anyone needing to be watching in real time. Every system in this guide offers a professional monitoring tier — make sure it's the one you actually select.

2. Medical Pendant and Panic Button Add-Ons — Know the Limits

A home security keypad panic button is useful, but it requires your parent to reach the keypad. A wearable pendant, worn on the wrist or around the neck, can be pressed from anywhere in the home — and some, covered in our medical alert systems guide, add automatic fall detection that doesn't require pressing anything at all. ADT and Vivint both offer bundled or add-on pendants; SimpliSafe, Ring, and Cove do not. If a wearable emergency button with fall detection is a priority, plan to pair whichever security system you choose with a dedicated medical alert device.

3. Contract Terms — No-Contract vs. Multi-Year Commitments

SimpliSafe, Ring Alarm, and Cove all operate month-to-month with no long-term contract, which matters if your parent's living situation could change — a move to assisted living, for instance. ADT and Vivint typically involve contract terms tied to financing the equipment or professional installation. Before signing anything on a parent's behalf, get the exact contract length, monthly cost after any promotional period ends, and early-termination fee in writing.

4. Cellular Backup — What Happens During an Outage

A system that only communicates over home Wi-Fi stops working the moment the internet goes down — whether from a storm, a router problem, or simply someone unplugging it by accident. Cellular backup keeps the system connected to the monitoring center independently of the home internet. It's included in Ring's Protect Plus plan and standard with ADT and Vivint's professional monitoring; confirm it's included (not an extra add-on) with whichever plan you choose, since it's one of the most important reliability features for a senior living alone.

5. Family Remote Monitoring

Every system in this guide includes a smartphone app that supports adding family members as secondary users. This means you can check whether the system is armed, see a log of when doors opened and closed, and get real-time alerts — useful both for catching an emergency early and for simply understanding your parent's daily rhythm from a distance without being intrusive about it.

6. Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Monitoring — Beyond Intrusion Detection

For an aging parent, monitored smoke and carbon monoxide detection often matters more than intrusion detection. A standalone smoke alarm just makes noise in the house; a monitored one alerts a staffed center that can call for help even if your parent can't get to a phone. This is included by default with ADT and Vivint's standard plans, while Ring requires the separate Smoke & CO Listener add-on. Confirm this is active on whichever system you choose — it's not something to leave as an afterthought.

7. DIY vs. Professional Installation

SimpliSafe, Ring, and Cove are designed for do-it-yourself installation, typically completed in under an hour with adhesive-mounted sensors — a good fit if an adult child can visit to set it up, or if the parent is reasonably tech-comfortable with some guidance. ADT and Vivint require professional installation, which costs more and means scheduling an appointment, but ensures sensors are placed correctly and that someone walks your parent through operating the system in person before leaving.

8. Insurance Discounts and UL Certification

Most US homeowners insurers offer a discount of 5% to 20% for a professionally monitored security system — often $75 to $300 a year in savings, and sometimes stackable with a separate senior or age-based discount. The key phrase to look for is UL-certified monitoring: UL (Underwriters Laboratories) audits central monitoring stations against UL 827, a standard covering staffing, facility redundancy, and response protocols, with annual re-certification required. Self-monitored, DIY-only setups typically don't qualify for the larger discount tiers. Ask your provider for a certificate of installation or monitoring to submit to your insurer, and ask your insurer directly which providers and certifications qualify for their maximum discount.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most important home security features for elderly parents living alone?
The most important features are 24/7 professional monitoring (not just self-monitoring), a simple keypad that's easy to arm and disarm, monitored smoke and carbon monoxide detection, cellular backup so the system stays connected during an internet or power outage, and remote app access so adult children can see arm/disarm activity and receive real-time alerts. A panic button on the keypad or a wearable pendant add-on is also valuable for a senior living alone.
Do home security systems detect falls?
No, not on their own. Standard home security sensors — door/window contacts, motion detectors, glass-break sensors — are built to detect intrusion, fire, and carbon monoxide, not falls. Fall detection is a feature of dedicated medical alert wearables, like the pendants covered in our best medical alert systems guide. Some security providers, including ADT and Vivint, now bundle a separate emergency pendant with their senior-focused plans, but true automatic fall detection still comes from a medical-alert-specific device, not the alarm panel itself.
Is professional monitoring necessary for seniors living alone, or is self-monitoring enough?
Professional monitoring is strongly recommended over self-monitoring for a senior living alone. With self-monitoring, the only alert goes to whoever has the app open on their phone — if a smoke detector triggers at 3 a.m. and no one is watching, nothing happens. With professional monitoring, a staffed monitoring center receives the alert, attempts to reach your parent, contacts a designated family member, and dispatches emergency services if needed, all automatically. Self-monitoring is fine as a supplement, but it should not be the only safety net.
Can adult children monitor a parent's home security system remotely?
Yes. SimpliSafe, Ring Alarm, ADT, Vivint, and Cove all offer smartphone apps that let you add family members as secondary users. You can see whether the system is armed or disarmed, get notified the moment a door opens or a sensor triggers, and in most cases view live camera feeds if cameras are part of the system. This makes it possible to keep an eye on a parent's home and daily routine from anywhere.
What happens to a home security system during a power or internet outage?
Modern systems are designed to keep working through outages. Most panels include a backup battery that keeps the system running for several hours during a power cut, and cellular backup (built into plans like Ring Protect Plus, and standard with ADT and Vivint) keeps the system connected to the monitoring center even if home Wi-Fi or a cut phone line takes down the internet. When shopping, confirm that cellular backup is included rather than an optional add-on, since it's essential for a senior living alone.
Will a monitored home security system lower my parents' homeowners insurance?
Yes, typically. Most US insurers offer a discount of 5% to 20% on homeowners insurance premiums for a professionally monitored security system, which on a $1,400 annual premium works out to roughly $75 to $300 in yearly savings. Insurers such as USAA and State Farm are commonly cited as offering some of the largest discounts. To claim it, ask your security provider for a certificate of installation or monitoring and submit it to your insurance company — self-monitored, DIY-only setups usually don't qualify for the full discount.

For most families setting up security for a senior parent, SimpliSafe is the right starting point. The lack of any contract means you can try it without a multi-year commitment, the keypad's hidden panic button and programmable key fob give your parent a discreet way to call for help, and DIY installation means you can have the whole system running during a single afternoon visit.

If cost is the deciding factor, Ring Alarm delivers genuine professional monitoring — including cellular backup — for less than any other option here, and it's the natural choice if your parent already has a Ring video doorbell. Families who want the reassurance of an established, professionally installed system with a dedicated medical alert line under the same provider should look at ADT, while Vivint stands out specifically for aging-in-place households thanks to its free pendant for customers 75 and older.

Whichever system you choose, remember that home security and medical alert coverage solve different problems — a security system protects the house and calls for help, while a wearable pendant protects your parent wherever they are and can detect a fall automatically. For full coverage, pair your chosen system here with a device from our best medical alert systems guide, and consider a video doorbell so your parent never has to open the door to a stranger without seeing them first.
