Holiday visits are often when families notice something has changed: a parent who lives alone has had a scare, the TV is louder than before, or the front door gets opened to anyone who knocks. Black Friday sales arrive at the same time, and most of what is advertised is not useful for an older parent.

Every pick below is sold in Canada and comes from one of our Canadian reviews or from the same research as our US guide. Where we could confirm a regular price in Canadian dollars from the maker or a Canadian retailer, we list it, so you can judge a sale. Some US picks, such as TV Ears and Audien hearing aids, are left out because we could not find them on Amazon.ca.

Quick tip: Several picks here are Amazon devices (Fire tablets, Fire TV, Ring and Blink). If your parent doesn't have Prime, start a free trial if they are eligible.

How to Tell a Real Black Friday Deal

1. Compare with the regular price, not the "was" price

The crossed-out price on a deal is whatever the seller chooses to show. Where we list a regular price, it comes from the maker's Canadian store or from a Canadian retailer, so it is a fairer yardstick. A price-history tracker such as camelcamelcamel shows what an item has actually sold for over recent months.

2. Check the exact model and generation

Older models often carry the biggest discounts. An Apple Watch SE is not necessarily the SE 3, Fire tablets come in more than one storage size, and Ring sells several battery doorbells with similar names. Match the model name in the listing title to the one in this guide.

3. Add up the monthly costs

For medical alerts, GPS trackers and cameras, the monthly plan can cost more in a year than the device. A discount on the hardware means little if the plan is expensive or the deal locks you into a long term.

4. Check who is selling it

On Amazon.ca, look under the Buy button for "Ships from" and "Sold by". Some listings are from third-party sellers at higher prices than the maker's own store; Apple Canada's prices above are a useful check on Apple products.

5. Check the delivery date and where it ships from

Some Amazon.ca listings are fulfilled from outside Canada, which can mean a longer wait than you expect in the busiest shipping weeks of the year. If the item is a gift for a visit or for the holidays, check the delivery date shown at checkout before you order, not just the price. A slightly higher price from a listing that ships from Canada can be the better deal if it arrives on time and is simpler to return. The same goes for setup: if you plan to install a doorbell or set up a tablet during a holiday visit, order early enough that it is waiting for you.

6. Remember Boxing Day

Many Canadian retailers hold a second round of sales on Boxing Day, December 26. If a pick is not discounted on Black Friday and it is not urgent, waiting a month is an option. A medical alert is the exception: if your parent needs one now, do not wait for a sale.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Black Friday 2026 in Canada?
Black Friday is Friday, November 27, 2026, the same day as in the US. Canadian Thanksgiving is in October, so there is no holiday weekend attached, but many Canadian retailers and Amazon.ca hold Black Friday sales too. Cyber Monday is November 30, and Boxing Day on December 26 is another big sale day in Canada.
How do I know if a Black Friday price is a real deal?
Compare it with the regular price in this guide rather than the crossed-out "was" price, check that the model and generation match, and add up any monthly plan the product needs. A price-history tracker shows what the item has sold for on Amazon.ca in recent months. If the "deal" price is close to the regular price, there is no need to rush.
Do Canadian medical alert services have Black Friday offers?
They run their own offers on their own sites. On October 25, 2026, Lifeline Canada advertised free installation for new activations, with conditions including a 3-month term, and TELUS lists a lower monthly price for existing TELUS Home, Mobility or Koodo customers. Read the terms before signing up, and see our Canadian medical alert review to compare the services.
Is an AirTag a GPS tracker?
No. An AirTag finds things through Apple's Find My network, using nearby iPhones and iPads, not GPS. It works well for keys, wallets and bags, and needs an iPhone or iPad to set up and to find. For a parent who wanders, a GPS tracker or a medical alert with GPS is the safer choice; our Canadian GPS tracker review compares them.
Which of these picks need a monthly plan?
Medical alert services and the LandAirSea 54 need a monthly plan. Ring and Blink cameras and doorbells work without one, but saving and replaying video needs a plan. The Apple Watch SE 3, AirTags and tablets have no monthly fee. The Fire TV Stick is free to use, but paid streaming services cost extra.
Why are some picks from the US guide missing?
The US version of this guide also includes TV Ears headsets and Audien hearing aids, which we could not find on Amazon.ca on October 25, 2026, and US medical alert companies. For hearing help in Canada, see our Canadian hearing aid review; for medical alerts, our Canadian medical alert review covers services that operate here.
