Best Senior Cell Phone Plans in Canada 2026

A parent who only calls the family a few times a week does not need the same plan as one who video calls the grandchildren from the park. Most seniors are on a plan that is too big, or on an old one that costs too much. This guide helps you match the plan to how your parent actually uses the phone.

Canadian phone plans are confusing because the same three networks (Bell, Rogers and Telus) sell service under many brand names. Chatr, Lucky Mobile and Virgin Plus are cheaper brands owned by the big carriers, and they run on the same networks. If you already picked a phone, our guide to the best cell phones for seniors in Canada covers the devices. This page covers the plan.

We checked the plan details below on the carriers' own pages on September 29, 2026. Several of these prices are new-customer offers, and we say so where the carrier does. Some carriers block automated page checks, so we did not include prices we could not confirm ourselves. Those are listed near the end.

How Much Data Does a Senior Need?

Check what your parent uses before you pick a plan. On an iPhone, open Settings and then Mobile Service to see data used this period. On Android, look under Settings, Network and Data usage. A few months of history will tell you more than any guess.

These are our own rules of thumb, not carrier figures:

  • About 1GB a month: a parent who calls and texts, checks the odd message and is usually on home Wi-Fi.
  • About 5GB a month: a parent who uses maps, sends photos and watches the occasional video away from home.
  • 10GB to 15GB a month: a parent who video calls family often, streams music or has no Wi-Fi at home.

Buying more data than needed is the most common way families overpay. Buying too little is the most frustrating, because a phone that slows to a crawl is useless in an emergency. If your parent is unsure, start one step above what the numbers suggest. On a prepaid plan you can change it later. If your parent mostly video calls family at home, a Wi-Fi tablet may need no plan at all: see our best tablets for seniors in Canada.

Programs for Seniors on the GIS or Income Support

If your parent receives the federal Guaranteed Income Supplement, check these two programs before looking at anything else. They give far more data for the money than any regular plan we found.

Telus Mobility for Good

Telus runs a program for seniors who receive the GIS. There are two plans, both with unlimited Canada-wide talk and text and no contract or cancellation fees:

  • $25 a month: 10GB of 5G data, plus unlimited data at reduced speeds after that.
  • $35 a month: 20GB of 5G data, plus unlimited data at reduced speeds after that.

Your parent can bring their own phone or take a free certified pre-owned Samsung Galaxy S23 FE on a 2-year term. Telus also lists a $75 discount on a phone or accessory through Mobile Klinik.

Who qualifies: your parent must receive the GIS and provide their Old Age Security statement (the T4A slip). Telus does not state an age requirement. It says it responds to applications within one week, and mailed applications can take longer. You can read the details and apply on Telus's Mobility for Good page.

Rogers Connected for Success

Rogers has a similar program, but it is not aimed only at seniors. The mobile plans are 5G, with unlimited Canada-wide talk and text:

  • $25 a month: 10GB.
  • $35 a month: 15GB.

Who qualifies: Rogers says applicants must be receiving income or disability support, the federal GIS, or qualifying for rent-geared-to-income housing. The page does not mention an age requirement. Rogers says it reviews applications within two weeks. Rogers also sells discounted home internet through the same program, which can matter if your parent has no Wi-Fi. See the details on Rogers's Connected for Success page.

Which one? If your parent qualifies for both, compare coverage at their home address first. Telus offers more data at the top tier (20GB against 15GB), and the phone offer is a real saving if they need a phone. Rogers may suit someone who also needs cheap internet. Both are much cheaper than any regular postpaid plan.

Budget Prepaid: Chatr and Lucky Mobile

If your parent does not qualify for a program, prepaid plans from the big carriers' cheaper brands are the lowest-cost option we verified. They have no contract. The tradeoff is 4G speeds instead of 5G and less in-store help.

Chatr (Rogers network)

Chatr's plans include unlimited Canada-wide calling and unlimited texting to Canada, the US and other countries, plus voicemail. The data amounts below include a bonus you receive when you pay by autopay:

  • $21 a month: 1GB (0.5GB plus 0.5GB autopay bonus).
  • $25 a month: 5GB (includes a 1GB autopay bonus).
  • $30 a month: 15GB (includes a 5GB autopay bonus).
  • $35 a month: 30GB (includes a 5GB autopay bonus).
  • $159 a year: 40GB, which works out to about $13 a month.

Chatr says these prices apply to new customers. Check Chatr's plans page for the current rates.

Lucky Mobile (Bell network)

Lucky Mobile plans include unlimited Canada-wide calling, unlimited Canada-wide and international texting, voicemail, call display and call waiting. The data amounts include a bonus when you set up Auto Top-Up:

  • $21 a month: 1GB (500MB plus 500MB bonus). Lucky lists this as a new-activation offer, so confirm it is still available.
  • $25 a month: 5GB (4GB plus 1GB bonus).
  • $30 a month: 15GB (10GB plus 5GB bonus).
  • $35 a month: 30GB (25GB plus 5GB bonus).

After you use up the data, speeds drop to 128 Kbps, which is enough for a text message but not much else. See Lucky Mobile's plans page for current prices.

Which one? The prices are almost identical, so choose by network. Chatr uses Rogers and Lucky uses Bell. Ask a neighbour or another family member what works in your parent's area, and look at each carrier's coverage map for the home address.

Postpaid: Rogers 65+, Bell and Virgin Plus

Regular monthly plans cost a lot more than prepaid, but include 5G, larger data amounts and, on some, US roaming. They only make sense for a senior who travels or wants a big carrier's stores and support.

Rogers 65+ plans

Rogers lists three plans for customers who are 65 or older:

  • 5G+ Essentials 65+: $85 a month regular price, or $65 after autopay, with 100GB of high-speed data. It includes US data, talk and text for 24 months.
  • 5G+ Popular 65+: $100 a month, or $80 after autopay, with unlimited high-speed data and coverage in the US, Mexico and the Caribbean for 24 months.
  • 5G+ Ultimate 65+: $115 a month, or $95 after autopay, with unlimited high-speed data.

The conditions matter. You need government-issued ID showing proof of age. The plans are for new activations only, on up to two lines, when you bring your own phone. Rogers also lists an extra $5 a month seniors' discount on the first line. Check the current offer on Rogers's 65+ page.

Even at $65, this is three times the price of a $21 prepaid plan. It is worth it for a parent who travels to the US in winter, or who wants to walk into a store when something goes wrong. It is not worth it for a parent who calls three people a week. If most calls are made at home, an amplified cordless phone may cost less than any plan.

Bell

Bell's plan page for customers bringing their own phone lists Select 60GB at $50 a month and Select 100GB with US roaming at $55, both with a streaming bundle credit. Its Ultra unlimited plans are $70 and $85 a month. The page did not show a senior-specific offer when we checked. Bell's own senior deals may exist in stores or through promotions, so ask before you sign up.

Virgin Plus (Bell network)

Virgin Plus lists a bring-your-own-phone plan with 20GB of data for $40 a month with autopay credit. It is for new activations only. It sits between the prepaid brands and the full-price Bell plans.

How to Switch Without Losing the Number

  1. Check the phone is unlocked. A phone locked to one carrier will not work with another carrier's SIM. Ask the current carrier to unlock it.
  2. Check the coverage map. Enter your parent's home address on the new carrier's coverage map, and check the places they go most often.
  3. Keep the old service active. Do not cancel until the new SIM works and the phone number has moved across.
  4. Move the number. Give the new carrier the old account number and the phone's details. The transfer usually finishes within a day.
  5. Set up the phone before handing it over. Enlarge the text, add family as quick-dial contacts and test calling 911.

For seniors who are on a cheaper plan already but worry about safety, a medical alert device can matter more than the phone plan. See our medical alert systems guide for Canada.

Other Budget Brands We Could Not Verify

Koodo, Fido, Freedom Mobile, Public Mobile and Fizz all sell low-cost plans in Canada. Their websites blocked our automated price checks or did not show clear prices, and we do not publish prices we could not confirm. Compare them with the plans above before you decide, especially if your parent lives somewhere they have stronger coverage. Fizz and Public Mobile in particular often come up as low-price options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest cell phone plan for seniors in Canada?
For a senior who mostly makes calls, the cheapest options we verified are prepaid plans from Chatr (Rogers network) and Lucky Mobile (Bell network). Chatr starts at $21 a month for 1GB and Lucky Mobile at $21 a month for 1GB, with unlimited Canada-wide calling. Both prices are for new customers, so confirm the current rate before signing up. Seniors who receive the GIS can get 10GB for $25 a month through Telus Mobility for Good.
Do Canadian carriers offer a senior discount?
Rogers has dedicated 65+ plans: 5G+ Essentials 65+ is $85 a month, or $65 after autopay, with 100GB of data. They require government ID showing proof of age and are for new activations only, on up to two lines, when you bring your own phone. Telus and Rogers also run programs for low-income customers, including seniors on the Guaranteed Income Supplement. Bell's plan page does not show a senior offer.
Who qualifies for Telus Mobility for Good for seniors?
Applicants must receive the Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) and provide their Old Age Security statement (T4A). The plans are $25 a month for 10GB or $35 a month for 20GB of 5G data, with unlimited Canada-wide talk and text and no contract. Telus offers a free certified pre-owned Samsung Galaxy S23 FE on a 2-year term, or you can bring your own phone. Telus says it responds to applications within one week.
Who qualifies for Rogers Connected for Success?
Rogers says applicants must be receiving income or disability support, the federal Guaranteed Income Supplement, or qualifying for rent-geared-to-income housing. The mobile plans are $25 a month for 10GB or $35 a month for 15GB, with unlimited Canada-wide talk and text. The program is not limited to seniors, and Rogers says it reviews applications within two weeks.
How much data does a senior need?
As a rule of thumb, a senior who mainly calls and texts and is often on home Wi-Fi can manage on 1GB a month. If they use video calls, maps and photos away from home, 5GB to 15GB is a safer range. Check how much data the phone actually used in the last few months before choosing.
Can my parent keep their phone number when switching plans?
Yes. Canadian carriers must let customers move their number, and the transfer usually finishes within a day. Have the old account number ready, and do not cancel the old service until the new phone is working with the old number.

Our Recommendation for Most Canadian Families

If your parent receives the GIS, apply for Telus Mobility for Good first: $25 for 10GB is hard to beat, and the free phone helps if they need one. Also apply to Rogers Connected for Success if they need cheap internet, since it costs nothing to apply.

If they do not qualify and mostly make calls, a Chatr or Lucky Mobile plan at $21 a month is the sensible choice. Pick whichever network covers their home better. Use the 5GB tier for $25 if they send photos or use maps.

Only look at Rogers 65+ or Bell postpaid plans if your parent travels to the US or wants a store to walk into. Whatever you choose, check the price on the carrier's page on the day you sign up, because promotions change often.
