Life Alert is often the first name families think of because of its TV ads. Brand recognition and value are different things, and this guide compares it with Medical Guardian using published terms.

Life Alert has been running the same "Help, I've fallen and I can't get up!" commercials since 1987. That phrase is so well known that many families assume Life Alert is the obvious choice. Whether it is depends on price, contract terms and features.

We have not tested either service ourselves. This comparison uses the prices and terms published in NCOA's Life Alert review and NCOA's Medical Guardian review, which are independent reviews. Companies change prices and terms, so confirm each one with the provider before you buy.

Response Time: Head-to-Head

Response time, the wait between pressing the button and hearing a person, is one of the most important things to ask about. We could not find independently tested response times for either company, and we have not tested them ourselves, so we do not rank them on speed.

Ask each company how it measures response time, whether its monitoring centre is independently certified, and whether it offers a trial or return period so you can test the button with your parent before committing.

Monthly Cost Comparison

Life Alert does not post its prices online, so you have to call for a quote. NCOA's Life Alert review lists plans from $69 to $109.85 a month depending on the package, with a $248 activation fee for the base system.

Medical Guardian's pricing page says MGHome Cellular starts at $34.95 a month, with monthly, quarterly or annual billing. NCOA's Medical Guardian review listed it from $38.95 a month plus a one-time $149.95 equipment fee, so the two sources differ; ask for the full first-year cost in writing. If neither monthly fee works for your budget, see our guide to no-monthly-fee options.

Contracts and Cancellation

NCOA reports that Life Alert requires a three-year contract. Seniors' health and living situations can change quickly, so ask for the cancellation terms in writing and find out what happens if your parent moves or needs to stop service.

Medical Guardian's FAQ says it does not require long-term contracts and does not charge equipment, activation or cancellation fees, and that it gives pro-rated refunds for unused service. But its MGHome Cellular page carries a footnote saying "cancellation fees may apply", and its policies page says equipment you have not purchased must be returned, that lost or damaged equipment can cost up to $350, and that promotional discounts are forfeited if you change the order or billing cycle within 12 months. Ask for the exact terms in writing. Month-to-month terms are common among newer medical alert companies, and they suit families who are not sure what the next year will look like.

Fall Detection

Automatic fall detection can matter for a senior who lives alone. If your parent falls and is unconscious or disoriented, they may not be able to press the help button, so automatic detection is what can still call for help.

Medical Guardian lists fall detection as a $10-a-month add-on (Medical Guardian's own page). NCOA reports that Life Alert does not offer fall detection, and Life Alert's site describes a button the user presses to reach a dispatcher. Ask Life Alert directly and get the answer in writing.

No fall detection system is perfectly accurate: it can miss some falls and can trigger false alarms. It is a safety net, not a guarantee.

Equipment and Coverage

Both services use a wearable help button and a base unit or mobile device that connects to a monitoring centre. Before you buy, ask each company which cellular network its device uses, whether it works without a landline, how waterproof the button is, and what the warranty covers. Cellular devices do not work in areas without coverage, so check the coverage for your parent's address.

Who Should Choose Each?

Choose Medical Guardian if:

  • You don't want to commit to a 3-year contract
  • You want a fall detection option ($10/month extra) and no long-term contract
  • Budget is a consideration: NCOA's listed prices are lower for Medical Guardian
  • Your parent may move, change living situations, or need to cancel service

Choose Life Alert if:

  • Your parent specifically asks for Life Alert by name, and the company confirms in writing the price, contract terms and features you need
  • You have a long-standing family connection to the brand

On the published terms we found, Medical Guardian looks like the better fit for most families because of its lower listed price, no long-term contract and fall detection option. If familiarity with the Life Alert name matters to your parent, get its price, contract and cancellation terms in writing and compare them with the alternatives.

Our Recommendation

Medical Guardian MGHome Cellular: lower listed price, no long-term contract and a fall detection add-on, based on published terms. Our top pick among the systems we compared, but we have not tested it ourselves.
