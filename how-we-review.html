How We Review Products

What our guides are based on, what they are not, and how we keep them honest.

The Short Version

TechForDad researches products; it does not lab-test them. We do not physically test the products we recommend, and we never claim personal experience we do not have. Our guides are built from manufacturer specifications, plan terms, published expert reviews, verified buyer reviews and current prices, and we recheck prices every month.

What "Researched" Means

Every guide follows the same steps:

  • Manufacturer details. We read the specifications, warranty, plan terms and prices on each maker's own website.
  • Published reviews. We read expert reviews and independent lab tests where they exist (for example, AARP's fall-detection testing of medical alert devices), and we look for problems that verified buyers report again and again.
  • Accessibility guidance. We weigh what matters for older users, such as screen size, volume, button size, ease of setup and whether a subscription is needed, using published guidance from health and accessibility organizations.
  • Marketplace listings. For products we link to on Amazon, we check that the listing is live, buyable and well rated before we recommend it.

When a claim comes from someone else's testing, we say so and name the source. When we could not find independent test results, we say that too.

How We Choose and Rank

We do not use a numeric score. Each guide picks a small number of products against the same plain criteria, and explains the trade-offs in the guide itself:

  • Ease of use for someone who is not comfortable with technology.
  • Total cost, including monthly fees, contracts and add-ons, not only the sticker price.
  • Reliability as reported by independent reviewers and buyers.
  • Support and return terms, such as warranty and trial periods.
  • Availability in the country the guide is written for.

Prices and Updates

Prices change often, so treat any price in a guide as approximate. We recheck prices and models every month against the maker's store or the retailer. The "Updated" date on a page changes only when we actually change the content. When a product is discontinued or a claim turns out to be wrong, we update the guide and add a dated note.

US and Canada Guides

US and Canada guides are written separately. Canadian guides use Canadian prices (CAD), Canadian retailers and Canadian plans and programs where they matter. US guides use US prices, US retailers and US programs. If a product or program is not available in one country, the guide for that country says so.

Our Use of AI Tools

We use software tools, including AI, to help gather specifications and prices and to draft guides. Those drafts are checked against the sources above before we publish them.

Health and Safety Information

Some guides cover products that relate to health or safety, such as medical alert systems, blood pressure monitors and hearing aids. Our guides are general information for shoppers and are not medical advice. A product cannot replace a doctor's guidance, an audiologist's fitting or an emergency service. Talk to a healthcare professional before relying on a device for a medical decision.

Money and Independence

We earn commissions when you buy through some of our links, including as an Amazon Associate. No company pays for its ranking, and a product that pays us nothing can still be our top pick. Read the full affiliate disclosure.

Corrections

If you spot a wrong price, a discontinued product or a claim that does not hold up, email hello@techfordad.com and we will check it and fix it. See also About TechForDad.
